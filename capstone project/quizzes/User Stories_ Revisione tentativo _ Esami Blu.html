Testo della domanda

An epic "Manage my shopping cart" is split into the following stories: A) "Build the cart UI," B) "Implement the 'add item' API endpoint," and C) "Connect the UI to the API." What is wrong with this splitting strategy? (Select all that apply)

Domanda 5 Scegli una o più alternative:

The story titles are not in the correct "As a..., I want..., so that..." format.

Incorrect. While true, the fundamental problem is the splitting strategy itself, not the naming convention of the resulting items.

It is a horizontal split along technical layers rather than a vertical split of user-facing functionality.

Correct. The split is by architecture (UI, API) instead of by user value.

None of the individual split stories deliver demonstrable value to the end user.

Correct. The user gets no value until all three are completed and integrated, which defeats the purpose of splitting.

The original epic was too small to need splitting in the first place.

Feedback

Correct.

This is a classic example of horizontal (technical) splitting instead of vertical (functional) splitting. None of the resulting "stories" (A, B, or C) deliver any value to the end-user on their own. A better, vertical split would be: 1) "Add an item to the cart," 2) "View items in the cart," 3) "Remove an item from the cart." Each of these is a complete, valuable slice of functionality.

Le risposte corrette sono: It is a horizontal split along technical layers rather than a vertical split of user-facing functionality., None of the individual split stories deliver demonstrable value to the end user.

Testo della domanda

When evaluating if a user story is "Independent" (the 'I' in INVEST), what potential problems are you looking for? (Select all that apply)

Domanda 6 Scegli una o più alternative:

The story cannot be completed without another specific story being completed in the same sprint.

Correct. This is a classic dependency issue that violates the 'Independent' principle.

The story is very large and will take a long time to complete.

The story can be worked on by only one developer on the team.

There is significant overlap in functionality with another user story, suggesting they should be combined or rewritten.

Correct. Overlap indicates that the stories are not distinct units of value and are therefore not independent.

Feedback

Correct.

An independent story can be developed, tested, and delivered on its own. Problems arise when one story has a hard dependency on another (it cannot be started until another is finished) or when stories are so tightly coupled that they must be delivered together. This creates planning and delivery bottlenecks.

Le risposte corrette sono: The story cannot be completed without another specific story being completed in the same sprint., There is significant overlap in functionality with another user story, suggesting they should be combined or rewritten.

Le risposte corrette sono: Split the epic into separate stories: one for registration, one for login, one for profile management, etc., Identify it as an epic and plan to break it down into smaller, estimable stories during backlog refinement.

Testo della domanda

Which of these user stories have an issue with being "Testable" (the 'T' in INVEST)? (Select all that apply)

Domanda 8 Scegli una o più alternative:

"As an admin, I want to receive an email notification when a new user signs up."

"As a user, I want to be able to reset my password via an email link so that I can regain access to my account if I forget my password."

"As a shopper, I want the product search to be better."

Correct. "Better" is completely ambiguous. What does it mean? More relevant results? Faster? This cannot be tested without clarification.

"As a user, I want the dashboard to load quickly so I don't have to wait."

Correct. "Quickly" is subjective and therefore not testable without a specific performance metric (e.g., "loads in under 3 seconds").

Feedback

Correct.

A story is testable if there is a way to objectively verify that it is "done." Stories with subjective or ambiguous language like "fast," "user-friendly," or "better" are not testable until those terms are defined with concrete, measurable acceptance criteria. For example, "fast" must be defined as "the page loads in under 2 seconds."

Le risposte corrette sono: "As a user, I want the dashboard to load quickly so I don't have to wait.", "As a shopper, I want the product search to be better."

A team is using Planning Poker to estimate the story: "As a shopper, I want to add an item to my cart, so that I can purchase it later." The estimates revealed are: 3, 5, 5, 8, 13. Based on this outcome, what are the most appropriate next steps? (Select all that apply)

Domanda 10 Scegli una o più alternative:

Calculate the average (6.8) and assign the story a size of 8.

Immediately re-vote without any discussion.

Ask the person who estimated '13' to explain what complexities they foresee.

Correct. The highest estimator should explain their reasoning, as they may be aware of risks or hidden work others missed.

Ask the person who estimated '3' to explain their reasoning.

Correct. The lowest estimator should explain their perspective, as they may have seen a simpler solution.

Feedback

Correct.

In Planning Poker, a wide divergence in estimates (like 3 vs. 13) signals a difference in understanding. The correct procedure is for the team members with the highest and lowest estimates to explain their reasoning. This discussion clarifies assumptions about complexity, risk, or scope, leading to a more aligned re-vote. Simply averaging the numbers or picking the highest one misses the point of the collaborative exercise.

Le risposte corrette sono: Ask the person who estimated '3' to explain their reasoning., Ask the person who estimated '13' to explain what complexities they foresee.

Testo della domanda

What are the likely consequences of having vague, missing, or poorly written acceptance criteria for a user story? (Select all that apply)

Domanda 11 Scegli una o più alternative:

The implemented feature may not meet the Product Owner's expectations, leading to rework.

Correct. This is a very common outcome of ambiguity.

The team will finish the sprint early due to the lack of detailed requirements.

Difficulty in testing the feature properly, leading to more bugs escaping to production.

Correct. Testers can only test what is clearly specified.

Significant delays and debates about whether the story is truly "done".

Correct. Without a clear checklist, "done" becomes a matter of opinion.

Feedback

Correct.

Poor acceptance criteria are a recipe for problems. When the definition of "done" is unclear, developers may build the wrong thing (rework), testers won't know what to test (bugs), and the story is likely to be rejected during the sprint review because it doesn't meet the Product Owner's unstated expectations. This all leads to wasted effort and delays.

Le risposte corrette sono: The implemented feature may not meet the Product Owner's expectations, leading to rework., Significant delays and debates about whether the story is truly "done"., Difficulty in testing the feature properly, leading to more bugs escaping to production.

What are the primary purposes of writing acceptance criteria (AC) for a user story? (Select all that apply)

Testo della domanda

A manager asks, "Why do you use story points instead of just estimating in hours? A 5-point story could take one developer a day and another developer two days." Which are valid reasons to prefer relative estimation (story points) over absolute estimation (hours)? (Select all that apply)

Domanda 13 Scegli una o più alternative:

Story points are a team estimate of overall effort, not one person's time, which accounts for variations in individual skill and speed.

Correct. Story points are a whole-team measure, making them more resilient to changes in team composition or individual availability.

Story points can be directly converted to a monetary value for budgeting.

Relative sizing is faster and easier for humans, who are better at comparing things than at predicting absolute futures.

Correct. It's easier and more accurate to say "A is bigger than B" than to say "A will take exactly 12.5 hours."

Story points capture complexity, uncertainty, and risk in addition to the volume of work, while hours only capture duration.

Feedback

Partially correct.

Relative estimation with story points is preferred because it separates effort from duration and accounts for the whole team's work, not just one person's. It acknowledges that complexity, uncertainty, and effort are distinct from the time it takes. This leads to more stable estimates (velocity) over time, as the team's shared understanding of a "point" remains consistent even as individual speeds vary.

Le risposte corrette sono: Story points are a team estimate of overall effort, not one person's time, which accounts for variations in individual skill and speed., Relative sizing is faster and easier for humans, who are better at comparing things than at predicting absolute futures., Story points capture complexity, uncertainty, and risk in addition to the volume of work, while hours only capture duration.

Testo della domanda

A team determines that a story, "As a user, I want to pay for my order," is too large for one sprint. Which of the following represent good topics for a discussion on how to split this story vertically? (Select all that apply)

Domanda 15 Scegli una o più alternative:

"What is the simplest 'happy path' we can deliver first? Maybe we can defer handling expired cards to a second story." (Splitting by scenario complexity)

"Could we split this by workflow step? The first story could be 'Enter payment details and validate them', and the second could be 'Submit payment and show confirmation'."

Correct. As long as each step provides some form of value or learning, this is a valid splitting strategy.

"Can we first implement payment with just Visa cards, and add Mastercard and Amex in a later story?" (Splitting by business rule variation)

"Let's have one story for the front-end payment form and another story for the back-end payment processing API."

Incorrect. This is a horizontal, technical split. Neither story delivers value to the user on its own.

Feedback

Partially correct.

When splitting a large story, the goal is to find smaller, valuable, end-to-end slices. For a payment story, this often means splitting by workflow steps or by variations in data. A great starting point is to implement the simplest possible success path first (e.g., pay with one type of credit card). More complex paths (different card types, error handling) can be split into subsequent stories.

Le risposte corrette sono: "Can we first implement payment with just Visa cards, and add Mastercard and Amex in a later story?" (Splitting by business rule variation), "What is the simplest 'happy path' we can deliver first? Maybe we can defer handling expired cards to a second story." (Splitting by scenario complexity), "Could we split this by workflow step? The first story could be 'Enter payment details and validate them', and the second could be 'Submit payment and show confirmation'."
